Check it yourself

Download a day's .json and .json.ots files from the table (or from track_record/ in the repository), then:

sha256sum 2026-10-05.json            # = the sha256 of that day in index.csv
ots verify 2026-10-05.json.ots        # needs pip install opentimestamps-client; prints the Bitcoin block and its date

Each file also holds previous_sha256, the hash of the day before, so changing one day breaks every later one. A fresh proof is "pending" for a few hours, until a Bitcoin block confirms it; the daily job then completes it. Git commit dates are shown nowhere on this page: they can be rewritten, the Bitcoin anchor cannot.

Method and limits

What is measured. A stress episode starts when US equities (SPY) fall 10% from their 1-year high or when their volatility breaks above its historical 95th percentile. This rule was frozen on 2026-10-01 before any result was seen; its SHA-256 is printed in every published day, and the code refuses to run with any other version.

The alarm turns on when the detector score stays above 50% for 3 days. P(stress) is a separate, calibrated number: when it says 30%, stress followed about 3 times in 10 in the past. The alarm is tuned for speed, the probability for honesty.

Limits. A dozen or so crises since 2009 are few, and the median moves a lot with one of them. Negative delays mean the alarm was already on, not that the crisis was foreseen. The detector watches the same market the rule dates episodes on, so a delay near zero means "on the day it started". Market prices are not published here (data licences): only the model's outputs, episode dates and drawdowns.
